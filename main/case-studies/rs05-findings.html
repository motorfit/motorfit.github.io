RS05 results and remaining errors#

The selected M1 model predicts most of the recorded tracking motion with small position error. The release experiment exposes behavior that this model does not fully explain. Both observations are necessary when deciding where to use it.

For the original-friction CPU case, the worst final-test record has about 5.389 mrad position MAE: the 200 g lift-and-release run. An average of 2.220 mrad therefore does not mean every trajectory stays within that error.

These figures come from the full archive’s results/replay/mjlab317/*/metrics.json. Each folder’s protocol.json records the model hash, timestep, package versions and solver settings. The three-record quickstart is a smaller subset and has different aggregate statistics.

Agreement between engines#

The matching CPU/GPU results support consistency of the tested M1 implementation and solver settings. Newton also provides an integration path through SolverMuJoCo; this does not constitute an independent Featherstone comparison.

The later 3.17 study includes the required NoSlip settings. Limitations found in an earlier MJWarp environment should not be attributed to that later run. Results from different environments remain separate in the archive.

What happens after release#

In the representative 200 g release, the measured arm retreats by about 2.714 mrad under the study’s specified processed-signal metric. Frozen M1 predicts no such retreat and has a stopping error near 7.116 mrad.

Changing the modeled disable event to the raw STOP-send time reduces that stopping error to about 7.068 mrad. It improves early release alignment, but leaves the missing retreat and most of the stopping bias unexplained. Timing alone is therefore insufficient for this case.

The evidence is stored under results/release-alignment/ and results/comparisons/. STOP-send and reply times are observed communication events; they do not directly identify when phase current vanished.

Follow-up model investigations#

Dahl/LuGre friction, elastic and sensor/filter candidates improved some tracking metrics, but did not jointly explain release recoil and stopping across the tested loads. They were not promoted to replace the formal M1 model. These later analyses are diagnostic work using observed residuals, rather than a new untouched-test model-selection result.

The 500 g peak-to-stop motion includes a much larger backswing. It should not be interpreted as the same small-retreat regime measured in the 200 g case. Compare results only when signal processing, event definitions and measurement windows match. The generic replay tool’s release-window submetrics use the historical RS05 windows: 2–6 s active, 6–8 s release, and 8 s onward rest. New trajectories need metrics aligned to their own actual events.

Scope of the result#

The parameters apply to RS05 UID 542b38001c443916, the measured fixture and the recorded operating conditions. Other motors, changed mounting, different firmware and robot contact tasks need independent evaluation.

CAN torque is a nominal firmware value, and static register inspection does not supply a time-resolved current measurement during motion. The study identifies an effective mechanical model; it does not separately measure electrical constants or establish a physical explanation for every residual.

Use the evaluation guide to apply the same distinction between tracking accuracy, implementation consistency and unexplained mechanics to a new motor.
